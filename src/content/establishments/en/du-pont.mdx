The oldest restaurant in Zermatt, at least according to them. Unfortunately, oldest doesn't mean best.

## Food

A small menu, heavily focused on cheese: fondue, sandwiches, macaroni. If all you want is some hot cheese, like we did, this is a solid option, though the fondue we chose wasn't the hit we expected. It does offer several other fondue "flavors" like tomato, champagne with truffle, and chili, which all sound interesting.

## Location & Atmosphere

A very small, cabin-like place that tries to blend the fancy with the casual, resulting in a peculiar setting that I didn't dislike, but wasn't a huge fan of either; the neon purple light outside is kind of ugly, though. You can sit inside or outside and enjoy the town and the mountain views, and they have those fire heater things so you can be on the terrace at any time.

My bench got kind of uncomfortable after a while, but they have the best, top-quality paper napkins I've ever seen in a restaurant.

## Service

Very good, actually. Our waiter was nice, talkative without being annoying, and the food came out in a reasonable amount of time.

## Price point

Zermatt is already expensive, even by Swiss standards, but taking into account the food we got to try and the aesthetics of the place, I'd say du Pont is overpriced for what it offers.

## Verdict

It's kind of cool that it's the oldest place in Zermatt, and the food options are what you'd expect from a "classic" Swiss spot, but it was a little overpriced and I didn't vibe with the aesthetics.
