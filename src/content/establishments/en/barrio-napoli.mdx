I love Barrio Napoli. I remember watching them slowly build up their little restaurant, it must have had no more than 3 tables. I was there opening day, and ever since then I've been a fan returning at least once a month. 

## Food

Everything is top quality, most ingredients are imported from Italy, and they are prepared very meticulously. I know that the dough is fermented for at least 48 hours, and it shows. The pizzas are amazing, with some classic flavors and toppings, and others slightly less conventional but none less impressive. I love that chef Luigi is always trying to outdo himself by introducing a pizza of the month -- they're not all a massive hit, but it keeps things fresh and interesting.

Even though their pizzas are the stars of the menu, everything else is worth trying at least once. Pastas are cooked to perfection with some excellent sauces, and the appetizers have surprised me on more than one occasion.

**Note:** I have never, and don't think I will *ever*, try to eat from Barrio Napoli as takeout or delivery as I fear the quality of the food will drop dramatically during its transportation to its destination. Barrio Napoli should be enjoyed at the establishment.

## Location & Atmosphere

I'm going to be honest, I don't like the actual place. The decor is all over the place and sometimes even tacky, like with the several posters, pictures, and paintings of Maradona portrayed as a literal god. I respect chef Luigi for making his restaurant his own, but it is not my style. Also, the place feels very crowded and loud, which one could argue mimics the Italian lifestyle, but again, just not my style.

However, I can (and do) forgive all those things because the food is *that* good.

## Service

It is good, but that may also be because a lot of the staff is already familiar with me. They are really kind, polite, and smiling. The speed at which the food comes out, however, will vary a lot. Sometimes it will be ready before you know it, and other times you'll end up waiting 30 minutes for your pizza because they fumbled your order or didn't plan the oven queue properly.

But again, the food is *so* good that on the (not so common now) occasions when they take ages to bring it out, it's easy to forgive them.

## Price point

A little bit on the expensive side, but that's expected given the quality of the ingredients. Most of the time it's worth it, but with some wildcard dishes like the pizza of the month, sometimes you can feel like you overpaid.

## Verdict

Probably my favorite pizzas in Mérida right now, but everything else is amazing too. It can be a bit expensive, but it is worth it. Unfortunately the decor is not my cup of tea.
